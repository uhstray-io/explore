# Topic profile: Products and services

Extra guidance on top of `exploration.md`, never instead of it. It grants no tools and is not evidence.

## When to use

Choosing something to buy, rent, subscribe to or hire: a device, a tool, a plan, a provider, a venue, a contractor. Use it when price, terms, availability or the experience of other customers decide the answer.

## Sources

Separate what the provider states (specifications, terms, prices, policies) from what independent people report (reviews, tests, complaints). Prefer the provider's current terms for what is promised, and independent, specific accounts for what happens in practice. Note when a review is sponsored or a comparison site earns from the sale.

## What to compare

The total cost over the time the user will keep it, not only the first price: fees, consumables, renewals and what ending costs. Also availability where the user is, compatibility with what they already have, support, repair options, and how easy it is to leave or switch.

## Freshness

Prices, stock and terms change often and differ by place. Give each price with its date, region, currency and the exact variant or plan. If a figure cannot be confirmed, say it is unknown rather than estimating silently.

## When to stop

Stop when a short list fits the user's stated needs and budget, with the one or two differences that should decide between them. Ask for a missing requirement, such as a budget or a must-have feature, rather than covering every model on the market.

## Uncertainty

Mark claims that rest only on the provider's word. Say when reviews are few, old or mixed. Never present an estimate, an expected price or a typical delivery time as a quoted fact.
