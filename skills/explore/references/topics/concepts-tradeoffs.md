# Topic profile: Concepts and design trade-offs

Extra guidance on top of `exploration.md`, never instead of it. It grants no tools and is not evidence.

## When to use

Understanding an idea or choosing between approaches: what a term means, how a method works, or which design, process or policy to adopt when several are defensible. Use it when the answer is a judgement about trade-offs rather than a fact to look up.

## Sources

Prefer sources that explain their reasoning and state their assumptions: standard texts, the people who introduced an idea, and careful critiques of it. Note when a source argues for one side. Distinguish a definition from an opinion about it.

## What to compare

Make the assumptions behind each approach explicit. Compare who bears each cost and when, what each makes easy or hard later, how reversible it is, and what a small experiment could show. Separate durable principles from current fashion or one community's practice.

## Freshness

Principles age slowly; practices, tools and prevailing opinions age faster. Date claims about what is common or recommended now, and do not present a trend as a principle.

## When to stop

Stop when the user can see the real choice: the few approaches that fit, what each trades away, and which question about their situation would decide between them. Several approaches may stay viable; say so rather than forcing a winner.

## Uncertainty

Label value judgements as judgements, and say whose values they reflect. Say whether a disagreement is about facts, which evidence could settle, or about priorities, which only the user can.
