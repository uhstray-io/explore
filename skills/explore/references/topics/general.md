# Topic profile: General

Extra guidance on top of `exploration.md`, never instead of it. It grants no tools and is not evidence.

## When to use

The default. Use it for mixed or unfamiliar topics, and for ideas that are not technical: a community project, a personal plan, a piece of creative work, an event, a habit. Choose it whenever no other profile clearly fits, or when the branch touches several kinds of question at once.

## Sources

Prefer sources close to the matter: the people, organisations or documents directly involved, then careful secondary accounts. Say who is speaking and what they stand to gain. Treat the user's own answers as the strongest evidence about their goals, constraints and taste, and as no evidence about the outside world.

## What to compare

Compare options by what the user said matters, in the user's words. Name the costs that are easy to miss: time, attention, money, other people's effort, and what becomes hard to undo. When the topic lacks shared measures, describe the differences plainly rather than inventing a score.

## Freshness

Note when a fact can change, such as opening hours, rules, prices or who runs something, and give the date you checked. Durable ideas and personal preferences need no date.

## When to stop

A mixed or personal topic rarely has one right answer to find. A clear framing of the choice, two or three workable directions, or the one fact only the user can supply is usually the point to stop.

## Uncertainty

Name the context you do not have, and do not imply specialist expertise. Say which conclusions would change for a different situation, such as a larger group, another place or a tighter budget.
